# Quarterly Big Pharma Ranking Review

Conduct a quarterly review of the global ranking of leading pharmaceutical companies by medicines and vaccine sales.

The objective is to compare their current positions, project their evolution over five years, identify the factors that could change the ranking, and update the commercial CRM platform landscape in Big Pharma.

## 1. Current ranking

Build the top 10 using the latest complete fiscal year and a homogeneous metric of net prescription medicines and vaccine sales.

Analyse at least the 15 largest companies or candidates to identify possible entries into and exits from the top 10.

Apply these rules:

- Include only pharmaceutical and vaccine sales attributable to each company.
- Exclude MedTech, Diagnostics, Consumer Health, Animal Health, distribution, services, CDMO, and other non-pharmaceutical businesses.
- Explicitly explain adjustments made for diversified companies such as Johnson & Johnson, Roche, Merck/MSD, and any other affected company.
- For every figure, identify the fiscal period, original currency, and source.
- Convert figures to US dollars using one homogeneous, documented method.
- Do not combine manufacturer-reported net sales with pharmaceutical spending, list prices, or market estimates from IQVIA or other sources.
- If fiscal periods are not fully comparable, explain the difference and its possible impact.
- Include acquisitions closed before the cut-off date. Treat announced but incomplete transactions only as future scenarios.

## 2. Quarterly momentum monitor

Keep the official complete-year ranking separate from a more recent monitor based on:

- YTD or LTM sales;
- constant-currency growth;
- current-year guidance;
- updated consensus;
- major franchise performance;
- recent launches, approvals, and clinical events.

This monitor anticipates movement but must not replace or be blended into the annual ranking.

Highlight companies whose annual position is stable but whose recent trajectory indicates acceleration or deterioration.

## 3. Five-year projection

Project the top 10 for the year corresponding to the latest complete fiscal year plus five years.

Use the most recent accessible forecasts from Evaluate Pharma, IQVIA, financial reports, analyst consensus, and other reliable sector sources.

For each projection:

- identify whether the figure is:
  - reported or guided by the company;
  - a third-party or consensus forecast;
  - an own estimate;
- do not combine forecasts built from incompatible metrics;
- do not describe a forecast as probability-adjusted unless the source does so;
- use sales or rank ranges when uncertainty is high;
- show a rank band when sales ranges overlap or the difference between companies is below 5%;
- assign high, medium, or low confidence;
- distinguish downside, base, and upside scenarios when relevant.

Do not present future positions as exact when the evidence only supports an interval.

## 4. Competitive factors

For every current or potentially relevant company, assess:

- organic growth of major franchises;
- recent and upcoming launches;
- clinical pipeline and potential blockbuster assets;
- approvals, regulatory delays, and relevant clinical results;
- patent expiries and loss of exclusivity;
- exposure to generics and biosimilars;
- mergers, acquisitions, divestments, licensing, and business development;
- guidance revisions and significant consensus changes;
- development of therapeutic areas including obesity and metabolism, oncology, immunology, neuroscience, rare diseases, and vaccines;
- commercial, manufacturing, and regulatory capacity to convert pipeline into sales.

Pay particular attention to Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, AbbVie, Johnson & Johnson, Roche, AstraZeneca, Merck/MSD, Novartis, Sanofi, Pfizer, and Bristol Myers Squibb. Include any other company that enters or threatens to enter the top 10.

## 6. Commercial CRM

Attribute a CRM platform only when explicit, sufficiently reliable public evidence exists.

Distinguish between:

- legacy Veeva CRM hosted on the Salesforce platform;
- Veeva Vault CRM;
- Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud;
- other corporate Salesforce uses unrelated to pharmaceutical commercial CRM;
- other commercial platforms.

Apply these rules:

- Do not treat legacy Veeva CRM as evidence of a Veeva Vault CRM decision.
- Do not treat a Veeva implementation on Salesforce infrastructure as a Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud decision.
- Do not infer a corporate decision from partnerships, commercial relationships, job postings, technical integrations, generic vendor references, or isolated use cases.
- Distinguish global decisions from deployments limited to a country, business unit, product, or function.
- Use “transitioning” when a migration has been announced but not completed.
- Use “mixed” when evidence shows meaningful coexistence of multiple platforms.
- Use “not public” when evidence is insufficient. This means absence of public evidence, not absence of a platform.
- For each attribution, record the platform, scope, source, evidence date, and confidence.
- One explicit primary source may be sufficient when it clearly identifies company, platform, and scope. If the only source is a vendor and it does not clarify scope, lower confidence or use “not public”.

Include a CRM evidence appendix so that the main table remains readable.
